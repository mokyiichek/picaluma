Everything you loved about Picasa

We loved Picasa. It was fast, simple and kept your photos on your own computer. When it was retired in 2016, nothing quite replaced it, so we built Picaluma: an independent, open-source app inspired by Picasa and written from scratch for today's Windows, Mac and Linux.

Instant search
Results appear as you type, across file names, folders, captions, tags and cameras.
Every folder on one page
Scroll your whole library, newest first, or browse it as a folder tree.
One-click fixes
I'm Feeling Lucky, crop, straighten and 30 effects. Undo any of it, any time.
Email, print and export
Resized copies for email, print layouts and contact sheets, watermarked exports.
Cloud drives welcome
OneDrive, Google Drive, Dropbox and NAS folders, without downloading everything.
Private by design
No account, no upload, no subscription. Your photos stay on your computer.
Runs on older computers
Windows 10 and 11, 64- or 32-bit, Intel and Apple Silicon Macs from macOS 10.15, and Linux. Light on memory, and it doesn't need a graphics card.
Watches your folders
New photos appear by themselves within seconds, with no rescanning.
Photos, RAW and movies
HEIC from iPhone, RAW from every major camera, AVIF, Photoshop files and movies, alongside JPEG and PNG.
The keys you remember
Picasa's keyboard shortcuts, from starring to zooming in with the arrow keys.

Over 700,000 photos listed in under 12 minutes, searched in milliseconds.

Tested across local, external, network and cloud drives.

Install

Windows

Run the installer. If Windows says it protected your PC, choose More info, then Run anyway.

Mac

Open the .dmg and drag Picaluma to Applications. It's notarized by Apple, so it opens like any other app.

Linux

Make the AppImage executable and run it, or install the .deb with your package manager.

On a Mac you can also install from Terminal:

curl -fsSL https://ahmok.com/picaluma/install.sh | bash
